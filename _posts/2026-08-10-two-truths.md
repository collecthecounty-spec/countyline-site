Two weeks across Europe surfaced a quality of life that made the bubble back home visible for the first time — and left room for both feelings to be true at once.

There's a particular kind of vertigo that comes from leaving somewhere familiar long enough to forget its rules, and then coming back to find the rules were never universal to begin with. London moved at a different clock. Rural Sweden moved at almost no clock at all. Both, in their own way, made the pace of home look like a choice rather than a fact of life.

This isn't a piece about which place is "better." It's about the strange discomfort of holding two truths at once: that home is genuinely good, and that something about it now reads differently than it did three weeks ago. Neither cancels the other out.
